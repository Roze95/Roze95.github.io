What we cover

Almost every continuous problem in this class is linear, and almost every finite replacement is a matrix. So the subject has two halves that keep meeting: the linear algebra of structured matrices, and the physics and analysis of the problems those matrices came from. Three arcs:

1 · Linear algebra and discretisation Lectures 1–11

An introduction to computational methods, then matrices and linearity, finite differences, boundary conditions, elimination and LU, subspaces, the delta function, eigenvalues, positive definiteness, the 1D heat equation, QR.

2 · Equilibrium and finite elements Lectures 12–17

Least squares, the ATCA framework, springs and masses, trusses, oscillations, finite elements in 1D, finite differences in higher dimensions.

3 · Fourier methods Lectures 18–24

Poisson's equation and 2D finite elements, Fourier series, the DFT and the FFT, the Fourier transform, convolution, and the singular value decomposition.

Text and computing

Text. G. Strang, Computational Science and Engineering (Wellesley–Cambridge Press). The typed lecture notes posted here are the authoritative statement of what is examinable.

Computing. Assignments with a computational part may be done in Python (numpy / scipy / matplotlib) or MATLAB, your choice. Reference solutions are provided in both. Attach all code you used.

Collaboration and AI

You are encouraged to discuss the problems with each other. All final write-ups must be your own, in your own words. State clearly in your submission who you worked with and what outside resources — including AI tools — you consulted.

Using an AI system in place of your own reasoning, without saying so, is a violation of the collaboration policy. Using one as a study aid and saying so is not.
