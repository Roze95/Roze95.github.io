Research

I build classical, table-top wave experiments that emulate quantum mechanics — turning abstract quantum dynamics into something you can measure in a water tank or a bouncing-droplet cell — and complement them with theory and work in quantum communications and ultracold matter.

Current focus (MIT Seed Fund, PI): an independent program on hydrodynamic quantum analogs and optical emulations, awarded $141,850 by the MIT School of Science Research Innovation Seed Fund (2025).

Themes

Surface gravity water waves as quantum analogs

Linear and nonlinear surface gravity water waves reproduce hallmark quantum behavior: amplitude and phase of wavepackets in a linear potential, projectile-like packet motion, accelerating solitary wavepackets, Talbot revivals and Akhmediev breathers, and diffractive focusing. These experiments make quantum dynamics directly observable in space and time.

Bohmian mechanics & quantum potentials of classical waves

Reconstructing Bohm trajectories and quantum potentials from measured classical wave fields — including the three-slit experiment in a linear potential and phase-space horizons in the Bohm formalism — to probe the interpretational structure of quantum mechanics with classical data.

Pilot-wave hydrodynamics

Walking and bouncing droplets at the MIT Applied Math Laboratory: the Aharonov–Bohm effect in pilot-wave hydrodynamics, hydrodynamic rotating black-hole analogs, quantum-like energy quantization from waves in periodic media, and the transition from non-local chaos to local predictability in memory-driven systems.

Quantum communications & QKD

Free-space and satellite-based quantum communications: scaling the dimensionality and distance of QKD with spatial modes of light, and hands-on B92 / six-state protocol implementations with pulsed lasers, including their translation into undergraduate experiments.

Ultracold atoms & molecules

Experimental ultracold matter at MIT: reconstruction of the Na–Li ultracold molecule laboratory and contributions to a next-generation dysprosium quantum-gas-microscope platform with dual-isotope Dy.
