About

I am a C.L.E. Moore Instructor of Applied Mathematics at MIT, working at the interface of fluid dynamics, wave physics, and quantum mechanics.

My research uses classical wave systems — surface gravity water waves and pilot-wave hydrodynamics — as experimentally accessible analogs of quantum phenomena. I study Bohmian trajectories, accelerating and self-shaping wavepackets, phase-space horizons, and emergent quantum-like behavior in driven hydrodynamic systems, alongside work in free-space and satellite-based quantum communications and ultracold atoms and molecules.

Before MIT I completed my Ph.D. in Physics at Tel Aviv University with Prof. Ady Arie, and postdoctoral work at the Center for Quantum Science and Technology and at the MIT Department of Physics & CUA.

Research interests

  • Hydrodynamic quantum analogs and pilot-wave dynamics
  • Surface gravity water waves as analogs of quantum mechanics
  • Bohmian mechanics and quantum potentials of classical waves
  • Accelerating wavepackets, Talbot revivals, and Airy pulse shaping
  • Free-space and satellite-based quantum key distribution
  • Ultracold atoms and molecules
